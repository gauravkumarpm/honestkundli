Read this first. The astronomy here is real and verified: planetary positions agree with NASA JPL's Horizons ephemeris to within a fraction of an arcminute for most bodies. The step from a planet's position to a claim about a person is a different matter. Astrology has no demonstrated predictive validity in controlled testing. The year-by-year section below computes the traditional framework -- dasha periods, solar returns, Muntha, transits -- and labels each with the meanings classical texts assign to it. Those labels are tradition, not forecasts, and nothing here should inform a decision about health, money, relationships or anything else that matters.

Method. Sun, Moon, nutation, sidereal time and obliquity from Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms (2nd ed.). Planets from Standish/JPL Keplerian elements (1800–2050 fit) with general precession applied to give longitudes of date. Sidereal positions use the selected ayanamsa; Rahu and Ketu are the mean nodes, as published panchangams use.

Accuracy (worst case vs JPL Horizons, 1930–2040, in arcminutes): Moon 0.13, Sun 0.41, Venus 0.49, Pluto 0.65, Mercury 0.74, Mars 0.79, Neptune 0.89, Uranus 1.64, Jupiter 6.03, Saturn 9.71. A nakshatra pada is 200 arcminutes wide, so even Saturn's error is under 5% of the narrowest unit shown. Where a body sits close enough to a boundary for that error to matter, it is listed under "Close calls".
